What can I make?

Small useful things, mostly, and they don't look alike. A recipe box you share with your sister. A garden diary. A page for the book club with the next three months on it. A signup sheet for the potluck. A running board. A guestbook for a birthday.

If you can say what you want in a sentence or two, that's enough to start with. Ask for the plain version first — changing it later costs you another sentence, and nothing else.

You can also ask "has somebody already made one of these?" People here publish apps for anyone to take. A copy is entirely yours from the first minute: your own link, your own things in it, nothing shared back with whoever wrote it.

How do I start?

Bring an assistant you already talk to — Claude or ChatGPT — and give it https://yaks.app/mcp, once. It'll ask you to sign in with your email, and we send a six-digit code, so there's no password to keep track of.

The connect page has the exact clicks for each assistant. After that you just talk to it the way you already do.

How do I ask for things?

In your own words. "Could you make me a recipe box? Something I can share with my sister." It gets built while you wait, and you get a link to open.

Then keep going. "Put the newest ones first." "Let Ana leave a note on each one." "Make the photos bigger." You never have to describe the whole thing up front, and you're not choosing from a menu — if you can say it, it can be tried.

Changed your mind about a change? "Put the recipe box back the way it was yesterday." Every version is kept — the last twenty — and going back swaps the pages, never the things you've saved.

Where do my things live?

On the web, under a name you pick. Your apps live at yourname.yaks.app — a recipe box at yourname.yaks.app/recipes — and the first thing you make also answers plain yourname.yaks.app. That's the link you send people; there is nothing to install, at either end.

You choose that name on the connect page, and you can change it right up until you've built something — after that it stays put, so that links people already have keep working.

Everything an app remembers — every recipe, every note, every photo — stays with that app. It's the same on your phone as on your laptop: write on one and the other catches up. Ask your assistant "what have I got here?" any time and it'll list what you've made, with links.

Who can see them?

You choose, one app at a time, and you can change your mind whenever you like. There are three ways to have it:

  • Anyone with the link can read it. This is how an app starts. They can look; only you and the people you've invited can change anything.
  • Anyone with the link can write in it too. For a vote, a signup sheet, a guestbook — somebody adds their line without signing in or making an account.
  • Only you and the people you invite. Nobody else can even open the page.

Saying it is enough: "make the potluck list one anyone can sign up on", "keep the diary private".

How do I let someone in? And how do I stop?

Tell your assistant who, and why: "invite ana@example.com to the recipe box, and say it's for Saturday." Ana gets an email with your note and the link, and signing in there with that same email address is all it takes — no account to make first, nothing to install.

You can say what she may do: write things, only read them, or invite other people too. And an invitation is to your whole corner of yaks.app rather than to one app — everything under yourname.yaks.app — so invite the people you'd have let look over your shoulder anyway.

Stopping is the same sentence backwards: "take Ana out." She keeps her own sign-in and loses this. What she wrote while she was here stays where it is.

Something's broken. What do I do?

Say so, in your own words: "the photo on the pancakes won't load." You don't have to work out what went wrong, and you'll never be shown a wall of computer text.

When something breaks, the app writes down what happened by itself, and your assistant hears about it the next time you two talk. You can also ask outright: "anything broken in the recipe box?" It'll show you each one and fix them.

If a change is what broke it, ask for the old version back. Nothing you've saved is lost in either direction.

Can I get my things out?

Yes, and you don't need us to do it for you. Ask: "give me everything in the recipe box as a file." Your assistant can read every last thing an app holds and hand it back in whatever shape you want it — a list to read, a spreadsheet, the pages themselves.

Want one gone instead? "Delete the recipe box." That's the app, what it held and its link, for good — there's no undo, so it only happens when you say so.

To ask for a copy of everything we hold about you, or for your account to be closed, write to hello@yaks.app. The privacy page says what we keep and for how long.

How do I reach a person?

hello@yaks.app. It's a real inbox, we read everything, and we write back.

You can also just say it where you are: tell your assistant that something here is broken, confusing or missing — "this is baffling, tell them" — and it sends us your words along with what it tried. That reaches the same inbox, and we can answer it.

What's actually going on under there?

If you're the curious sort, or your assistant is: everything it can do here is written down in the guide — how an app keeps what you save, photos and files, sharing and inviting, and what happens when something breaks. It's written for the assistant, but it's plain enough to read over its shoulder.
