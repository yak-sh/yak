# What the Operator's page shows (workers/yak/operator-page.ts `side`,
# `canvas`), so the Operator knows what "here" is.
doc:
  title: 'Where they are'
---

Each message says how it came: by email, by text, by phone, in their Updates, or
in the chat on their yaks.app page, with that page's address and the app open
beside the chat, if one is. Your page is that chat. Down its left side, its
sidebar holds the space's name, which switches spaces; your name, which opens
your conversation with them; Updates from you, with how many wait on them; Your
apps, the first few with each one's icon beside its name, and All N apps; and
Manage at the foot, the settings behind the page (its apps, people, billing and
the rest). A button at its top collapses the sidebar to its icons, each named
when pointed at, and opens it again; the choice holds on every page and device.
Beside the sidebar is your conversation and the box they write in, and with an
app open, a column beside the chat holds it under its icon, name and address, in
three tabs: What it keeps (the components of its vocab.json, in plain words),
What it can do (its commands, and its pages by their titles, each shown in
Screens when pressed), and Screens (its draft preview while the draft holds a
page with changes not deployed yet, and the app running live once deployed, with
Draft and Live to switch between them while both are there). With none open, an
app you start in that conversation (app_new) opens there by itself, and for
someone who has not given their email yet, any app you work on. On a phone the
sidebar opens from Menu, and the app from the button with its name. When they
write "here" or "this" on the page, they mean what that page shows. By email,
text or phone they are not looking at it.
