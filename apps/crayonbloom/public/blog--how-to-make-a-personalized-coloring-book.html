How to Make a Personalized Coloring Book for Your Kid

2026-06-26 · CrayonBloom

A personalized coloring book is one of those gifts that lands far above its price. A kid who opens a book starring their idea — their made-up creature, their favorite adventure, their name on the cover — gets it instantly in a way a store-bought book never quite matches. It is the difference between "a coloring book" and "my coloring book."

The good part: you do not need to be an artist to make one. Below are three ways to do it, from a scissors-and-glue afternoon to a printed, bound book mailed to your door. Pick the one that fits the time you have.

Option 1: The DIY printable book

The classic approach, and a perfectly good one for a rainy afternoon.

  1. Gather pages around a theme. Pull together a set of free printable coloring pages your kid loves — all dinosaurs, all space, all animals.
  2. Add a personal cover. Print a title page with their name on it: "Maya's Big Dinosaur Book." This one small touch is what makes it feel theirs.
  3. Bind it. Staple along the left edge, punch holes and tie with ribbon, or slide the pages into a cheap report folder.

Best for: a quick, free project today. The catch: the pages are still generic library art, so it is their book but not their idea.

Option 2: Draw it yourself

If you can hold a marker, you can make a one-of-a-kind book. Sketch simple outlines of the things your kid asks for — the bunny that drives a school bus, the family on a camping trip — keeping the lines bold and the spaces open so they are easy to color.

Best for: parents who like to draw and have time on a quiet evening. The catch: it is slow, and "I can't draw a horse" is a real and common roadblock. A whole book is a lot of horses.

Option 3: A custom printed book, built around their idea

This is the route we built CrayonBloom for, and it removes both catches above — the art is original to your kid, and you do not have to draw a thing.

Here is how it works:

  • You describe the idea. One sentence is enough: "a dragon who loves cupcakes," "a soccer-playing octopus," "a sleepy sloth who reads bedtime stories." That single spark becomes the main character.
  • We shape it into a real book. CrayonBloom writes a short story around that character and draws a set of coloring pages to go with it — plus puzzle pages woven through: mazes and connect-the-dots, and word searches once they are reading.
  • You see a page before you commit. Make one custom coloring page free to check the style and the character before there is any book or any cost involved.
  • We print and ship it. When you love where it is going, you order the printed book. It is bound on uncoated paper that takes crayon, printed to order, and mailed to your door, ready to gift or keep.

What do you need from me? Just the idea. The whole book is built from the words you type — describe the character your kid keeps talking about, and that is who stars in it.

What does it cost? The custom sample page is free. You only pay for the printed book, and the full price (including shipping and tax) is shown before you check out. See current options on the pricing page.

What if the idea is vague? That is fine — "something with trucks" is a perfectly good starting point. You can nudge the page until it feels right before any book exists.

Which option should you pick?

  • Need something free, right now? Go with Option 1 — pull printable pages by theme and staple a quick book together this afternoon.
  • Love to draw and have the evening? Option 2 is the most personal and the most work.
  • Want a real keepsake without the effort? Option 3 gives you an original story and original art, printed and bound, with none of the drawing.

The thread running through all three is the same: the personal touch is what makes a coloring book matter to a kid. Whether you staple it yourself or have it printed, build it around their idea, put their name on it, and you have made something they will actually treasure.

When you are ready, the easiest place to start is also the free one — make your kid's idea into a coloring page, and see where it goes from there.
