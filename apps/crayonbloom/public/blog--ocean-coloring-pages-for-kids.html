Invent a Sea Creature: There Is Still Room Down There for Your Kid's

2026-06-30 · CrayonBloom

Kids fall hard for the ocean, and the aquarium gift shop knows it. But the clownfish in the gift shop is the same clownfish everybody else has, and sooner or later your kid starts inventing their own — the one with too many arms, or the one that glows, or the one that delivers mail.

Here is the good part. They are guessing ahead more than they are making things up.

Most of the ocean's animals have no names yet

Scientists put the number of species in the ocean somewhere between 700,000 and a million. Two-thirds of them, at least, have never been discovered or officially described. Nobody has seen them, drawn them, or given them a name.

And the sea floor those animals live on is almost entirely unvisited: explorers have laid eyes on less than 0.001% of the deep ocean floor, an area about the size of Rhode Island.

So when your kid draws a creature nobody has ever seen, that is not a small thing to point out. There are hundreds of thousands of them down there, waiting for someone to notice.

Five questions that build one

Ask them in this order, and write the answers down in your kid's words:

  • How deep does it live? Near the sand where the light is, or so far down it has never seen the sun. Deep changes everything else about the answer.
  • How does it move? Swims, drifts, walks on the bottom, jets around backwards. This decides the shape more than anything else.
  • What does it eat, and how does it catch it? A creature with a plan is more interesting than a creature with teeth.
  • What is its one strange trick? The glowing, the extra arms, the color changing, the singing. Every good sea creature has exactly one.
  • What is it doing right now? Save this one for last. It turns a creature into a scene, which is what makes it drawable.

Name it, then let them defend it

Give it a name and the creature stops being a doodle. Two naming routes that work:

  • Describe it. Longarm, Blinkfish, the Nine-Legged Whistler. Say what it obviously is and stop there.
  • Name the finder. Your kid found this one, so their name is on the table.

Then ask a question they will have to answer in character: what does it do when it is scared? You will get more creature in ninety seconds than you would get from an hour of drawing.
