When a meeting's notice and minutes are due

Michigan's Open Meetings Act sets three dates:

  • Proposed minutes are available to the public within 8 business days after the meeting (MCL 15.269).
  • Approved minutes are available within 5 business days after the meeting that approves them (MCL 15.269).
  • A special meeting, or a regular one moved to a new date, is noticed at least 18 hours before it starts (MCL 15.265).

Type a meeting's date to see its dates

Business days skip weekends and Michigan's legal holidays. These are the dates the township site works out on every meeting's page, by the same calculation, and nothing you type here is sent to us.

Minutes drafted as the board meets

  1. Press Record when the meeting starts

    Press it on the meeting's page, on a laptop or phone in the room. The words appear as they are said. Pause during a closed session: nothing is heard or kept while paused. A meeting recorded on something else can be uploaded afterwards.

  2. The draft fills in as the meeting goes

    Each motion, who moved and seconded it and how it ended, and each member's vote on a roll call. Name the voices, and the draft says who spoke. Whatever the recording doesn't make plain is left blank and marked unsure.

  3. The clerk corrects it and posts it

    Play any line back from the recording, fix what needs it, and post the draft as the proposed minutes: residents read them on the meeting's page, and those who asked are emailed them. Once the board approves them, the clerk marks them approved. Nothing is posted without the clerk.

  4. The recording is kept as long as the law says

    Michigan's records schedule for townships keeps a recording made for minutes until they are approved, plus one day (General Schedule 25, item 25.201). Then the page offers to delete it, and nothing is deleted unless the clerk chooses to.

Only the township's editors hear the recordings and see the drafts.

Due dates on every meeting, and an assistant for the rest

Each meeting's page says what is due
“Proposed minutes due Friday, Nov 20”, counted from the meeting, and “posted” once they are.
A reminder before a date is missed
Three business days before minutes are due and not yet posted, the township's assistant emails the clerk. It reminds, and blocks nothing.
An assistant that keeps the site
Email or chat with it to put a meeting on the calendar, call one off, post a notice or add minutes someone sent as a file. It makes the change and writes back. On yaks.app it is called the Operator.
