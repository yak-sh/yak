From middleware
to engines.

Keep the side-effects. Remove the power to intercept actions.

Middleware can watch state, too. The architectural change is what an engine cannot do: delay, rename, or swallow an action on its way to the reducer. It watches committed state and can dispatch new actions, each through the same direct path.

      What I would change

      Constrain the interface.
      Keep the behavior traceable.

      01 / The mistake

      Giving side-effect code control over dispatch meant an action could be delayed, renamed, or swallowed before the reducer saw it. Tracing a name was no longer enough.

      02 / The change

      Remove the interception interface. Dispatch goes straight to the reducer. Engines observe committed state and dispatch separate actions to report their work.

      03 / The payoff

      Side-effects stay flexible: timers, uploads, retries, and result actions. Pending intent and progress can live in shared state, while the original action remains traceable.

Its dispatch is the store’s own function; an observer cannot replace or withhold the action it is reacting to.

      State observation is shared. Interception is the difference.

      Switch the middleware to “Watch state” and both runs have the same state and action history. That behavior is possible with middleware, but it depends on choosing not to intercept. The engine interface removes that capability. Engines can still dispatch new actions; they cannot change an action already being dispatched. This small example omits production concerns such as retries, cancellation, and duplicate-effect handling.
