What "print-ready" means

A phone screen forgives almost anything; a printed page forgives nothing. Whatever route you pick below, the press will hold your pages to the same four specs:

  • Resolution: 300 DPI at the printed size. For a 9×7-inch landscape page that's about 2784 × 2176 pixels. Chat-default image sizes fall well short: they look crisp on screen and soft on paper, and upscaling can sharpen a near-miss but can't invent detail that was never there.
  • Bleed: art runs past every edge. Presses print slightly oversized pages and trim about an eighth of an inch (3 mm) off each side. Backgrounds must overshoot the trim line; art that stops at the edge leaves a thin white rim where the blade landed.
  • Safe area: faces and words stay in from the edges. The same trim that removes the bleed can clip anything near it. Keep characters roughly 7–9% in from every edge and text a full 10% in; a clipped letter is far more noticeable than a clipped leaf.
  • Page count: about 32 interior pages. Most binderies need roughly 24–32 pages before a proper bound spine works at all. 32 is the classic picture-book length, and it's our minimum too.

One more decision before any printing: trim size, the finished page dimensions. Picture books are usually square (8.5×8.5 in) or landscape (9×7 or 10×8 in). Pick the trim first, because your images' aspect ratio has to match it; a stack of landscape pages can't become a square book without cropping every scene.

Getting the pages out of the chat

Download each page image at full size: the download button on the image itself, not a screenshot. A screenshot captures the image at your screen's resolution, which throws away most of the pixels the press needs. Name the files in reading order (page-01, page-02, …) as you go; sorting 32 near-identical watercolors by memory is worse than it sounds.

Your options for printing it

There are several ways to print a book like this, and the right one depends on how much of the production work you want to do yourself. All of these work; here's what each one asks of you.

Print-on-demand, do-it-yourself (Lulu, Blurb, and similar)

Print-on-demand services will bind a single copy of a full-color book, typically a print cost plus shipping for a 32-page softcover, depending on trim, paper, and cover. The catch is that they take finished files, not images: you assemble an interior PDF at the exact trim size plus bleed, and a separate wraparound cover PDF whose spine width you calculate from the page count and paper stock. Expect an evening in a layout tool and, if you're wise, one proof copy before the version you keep. If you enjoy that kind of production work, this is the cheapest path to a bookstore-grade object.

Photo-book builders (Shutterfly, Mixbook, and similar)

Drag-and-drop editors, no PDF required: the gentlest tooling of any route. If your story text is already painted into the art, full-bleed image pages carry the whole book cleanly. The result reads as a photo album rather than a picture book: thicker photo paper, album-style binding, often a logo page at the back. For some books that's fine; for a book meant to sit beside the ones on your kid's shelf, it shows.

A local print shop

The fastest way to hold a draft; many shops turn a job around in a day or two. Binding options usually run to spiral, staple, or perfect-bound on cardstock rather than a true casebound picture book, and they'll want a finished PDF just like the print-on-demand route. Good for proofing the book before committing to a nicer edition; rarely the keeper copy itself.

Common questions

What resolution do AI-generated images need to print well?

300 DPI at the final printed size. For a 9×7-inch landscape page with bleed, that's about 2784×2176 pixels. Ask for the size explicitly when generating; chat defaults fall short.

How many pages does a printed book need?

Most binderies need roughly 24–32 interior pages before a bound spine works. Plan for about 32, the classic picture-book length and our minimum. If your book came out shorter, a few new spreads are quicker to add than you'd think; the outline step of the guide shows how.

How much does it cost to print a book made in ChatGPT?

The price depends on your selected document, binding, quantity, destination and shipping. A PrintBound quote shows the complete total and expiry before you decide to buy.

My images are too small. Can upscaling fix them?

Partly. Upscaling sharpens an image that's already close to print size, and it's a step we run on every book. What it can't do is invent detail that was never there: a chat-default image blown up to full-page stays soft, and painted lettering suffers first. The reliable fix is regenerating those pages at about 2784×2176 pixels; the style-sheet method in the guide keeps the regenerated pages matching the rest of the book.
